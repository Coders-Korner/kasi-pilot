## Channels

Channels describe how KasiPilot will reach, onboard, support, and retain its customers. Since the primary users are informal traders, the product should combine **digital communication** with **trusted community-based distribution**.

## 1. WhatsApp

WhatsApp should be the main product channel.

Traders will be able to:

- Send text messages to record sales.
- Send voice notes to update stock.
- Receive transaction confirmations.
- Receive low-stock alerts.
- Request daily or weekly business summaries.
- Ask questions through the chatbot.

This reduces friction because users do not need to download a separate application or learn a complex interface. The WhatsApp Business Platform is designed to support customer engagement, conversational commerce, sales, and customer service through business messaging.[^1]

## 2. Web dashboard

The web dashboard will be the secondary channel for traders who want a visual overview of their business.

It can be accessed through:

- A mobile browser.
- A lightweight progressive web app.
- A shared device at a business-support centre.
- A desktop computer used by distributors or programme administrators.

The dashboard will show:

- Daily sales.
- Revenue.
- Stock levels.
- Low-stock products.
- Transaction history.
- Basic business performance.
- Readiness Passport information.

The dashboard should not be the main entry point for the trader. WhatsApp should remain the easiest way to submit information.

## 3. Distributor partnerships

FMCG distributors can help KasiPilot reach many traders at once.

The distributor can:

- Introduce KasiPilot to existing retailer customers.
- Include onboarding information with deliveries.
- Train field representatives to demonstrate the service.
- Encourage traders to use it for ordering and stock updates.
- Sponsor access for a group of retailers.

This channel is valuable because distributors already have relationships with spaza shops and regularly visit or communicate with them.

## 4. Trader associations and community networks

KasiPilot can partner with:

- Spaza-shop associations.
- Informal-trader organisations.
- Township business forums.
- Local entrepreneur networks.
- Community-based organisations.
- Cooperatives.

These groups can help with:

- Recruiting pilot users.
- Building trust.
- Arranging demonstrations.
- Collecting feedback.
- Identifying local business needs.
- Supporting users who need assistance.

Trusted local intermediaries are important because informal traders may be cautious about sharing business information with an unfamiliar technology company.

## 5. In-person demonstrations

The product can be introduced through practical demonstrations at:

- Community halls.
- Markets.
- Taxi-rank business areas.
- Trader meetings.
- Local business hubs.
- Wholesale and cash-and-carry locations.
- Entrepreneurship workshops.

The demonstration should be short and practical:

1. A trader sends a voice note.
2. KasiPilot records the transaction.
3. The trader receives confirmation.
4. The dashboard updates.
5. The system shows a low-stock alert or daily summary.

This makes the value easier to understand than a technical explanation.

## 6. ESD and government programmes

KasiPilot can be distributed through programmes that already support informal businesses.

Potential channels include:

- Small-business development agencies.
- Spaza-shop support initiatives.
- ESD programmes.
- Municipal enterprise-development offices.
- NGOs.
- Development-finance institutions.
- Township economic-development programmes.

These programmes may sponsor onboarding, provide training, or include KasiPilot as part of their business-support offering. Government support programmes for spaza shops already use delivery-channel partners and intermediaries to distribute funding, stock, training, and business support.[^2][^3]

## 7. Financial institutions and funders

Banks, alternative lenders, and funders can become both partners and distribution channels.

They may introduce KasiPilot to:

- Existing small-business customers.
- Loan applicants.
- Informal businesses preparing for funding.
- Businesses receiving ESD or grant support.
- Trader networks linked to their development programmes.

The product should not present itself as guaranteeing funding. Instead, it should help users build more reliable business records that may support future applications.

## 8. Digital marketing

KasiPilot can use affordable digital marketing channels to reach traders and partners:

- WhatsApp referrals.
- Facebook community groups.
- TikTok demonstrations.
- Local-language videos.
- Instagram.
- LinkedIn for distributors, banks, and ESD organisations.
- Email outreach to potential partners.
- A simple landing page.

The marketing message should focus on the practical benefit:

> “Send a voice note. Know your sales. Never forget what is running out.”

## 9. Referral programme

Traders can invite other businesses to use KasiPilot.

Possible incentives include:

- Free access for a limited period.
- Additional business reports.
- Discounted premium features.
- Recognition as a community ambassador.
- Airtime or data rewards, if financially feasible.

Referral marketing is suitable for a local business network because traders often know other traders, suppliers, and nearby shop owners.

## Channel strategy by customer

| Customer | Main channel | Purpose |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Informal traders | WhatsApp | Daily use, sales recording, alerts, support |
| Spaza-shop owners | In-person onboarding and WhatsApp | Demonstration and adoption |
| Distributors | Direct sales and partnerships | Sponsorship, retailer access, order insights |
| ESD programmes | Partnership proposals and pilot projects | Programme monitoring and business support |
| Banks and lenders | Institutional partnerships | Business-readiness and risk information |
| Trader associations | Community meetings and field agents | Recruitment and trust-building |
| Government agencies | Tender, programme, and partnership channels | Large-scale implementation |
| NGOs | Development partnerships | Outreach, training, and impact reporting |

## Recommended launch channel

For the first pilot, use a focused three-channel approach:

1. **One distributor or trader association** recruits the traders.
2. **In-person demonstrations** help users understand the product.
3. **WhatsApp** becomes the daily operating channel.

This is more realistic than trying to acquire users through online advertising alone.

### Pilot example

```text
Partner with one township trader association
                 ↓
Recruit 20–50 spaza-shop owners
                 ↓
Demonstrate KasiPilot in person
                 ↓
Onboard each trader through WhatsApp
                 ↓
Provide daily use and support through WhatsApp
                 ↓
Measure usage, accuracy, and business value
```


## Business canvas version

> **Channels:** WhatsApp Business Platform for daily interaction, a mobile web dashboard for reporting, distributor partnerships, trader associations, community organisations, in-person township demonstrations, ESD and government programmes, financial institutions, social-media marketing, and trader referrals.

## Short presentation version

> KasiPilot will reach users primarily through WhatsApp, supported by partnerships with FMCG distributors, trader associations, community organisations, ESD programmes, and small-business agencies. In-person demonstrations will build trust and help traders onboard, while referrals and local-language digital marketing will support growth. The web dashboard will serve as a secondary channel for visual reports and partner monitoring.
