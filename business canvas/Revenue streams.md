## Revenue streams

KasiPilot can generate revenue from the organisations that receive business value from better trader data, ordering, monitoring, and supplier development. The trader should ideally receive a free or subsidised basic service during the early stages to encourage adoption.

## Recommended revenue model

The strongest model is a **B2B2C model**:

```text
Distributor, bank, ESD programme, or funder pays
                         ↓
             KasiPilot supports the trader
                         ↓
          Trader records business activity
                         ↓
     Partner receives better business information
```

This is more realistic than depending only on informal traders paying a monthly subscription.

## 1. Distributor subscription fees

FMCG distributors can pay to use KasiPilot with their retailer network.

They may pay for:

- A monthly fee per active trader.
- Retailer onboarding.
- Order and stock visibility.
- Distributor dashboards.
- Demand insights.
- Automated stock and order alerts.
- Integration with existing distribution systems.


### Example

> A distributor sponsors KasiPilot for 100 spaza shops and pays a monthly fee for each active shop.

The distributor benefits by understanding which products are selling and when retailers need to restock.

## 2. ESD programme contracts

Enterprise Supplier Development programmes can pay for KasiPilot as a business-support and monitoring tool.

They may pay for:

- Trader onboarding.
- Business-readiness assessments.
- Progress monitoring.
- Monthly portfolio reports.
- Readiness Passports.
- Impact measurement.
- Business-development support.

This aligns with the need for ESD programmes to track the businesses they support and measure outcomes such as SME growth, jobs, and programme impact.[^1]

## 3. Readiness Passport fees

KasiPilot could charge organisations for generating and managing verified business profiles.

Potential buyers include:

- Banks.
- Funders.
- ESD programmes.
- Distributors.
- Corporate procurement teams.

The Readiness Passport could include:

- Trading history.
- Revenue trends.
- Sales consistency.
- Stock movement.
- Business activity levels.
- Compliance information, where legally and ethically appropriate.
- Owner-provided business details.

Important: the passport should be based on user consent and should not be presented as a guaranteed loan approval or official credit score.

## 4. Data and business-insights subscriptions

Distributors, wholesalers, banks, and development agencies may pay for aggregated insights such as:

- Fast-moving product categories.
- Demand trends.
- Stock-out patterns.
- Geographic demand.
- Retailer activity levels.
- Supplier-readiness trends.

This data should be anonymised and aggregated unless the trader has explicitly consented to identifiable information being shared.

For example:

> A distributor pays for a dashboard showing which products are most frequently sold or running out across a participating group of retailers.

## 5. Premium trader subscriptions

The basic version can remain free, while advanced features are offered through a paid plan.

Possible premium features:

- Advanced sales reports.
- Profit tracking.
- Multiple employees or shop users.
- Detailed inventory management.
- Exportable business statements.
- Automated weekly reports.
- Supplier price comparisons.
- Multiple business locations.
- More extensive historical data.

A possible structure could be:


| Plan | Target user | Features |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Free | New or individual trader | Basic sales recording and alerts |
| Premium | Growing trader | Advanced reports, profit tracking, exports |
| Business | Larger trader | Multiple users, branches, detailed inventory |
| Sponsored | Trader supported by a partner | Access paid for by distributor or programme |

Do not make individual subscriptions the primary model until user research proves that traders are willing and able to pay.

## 6. Supplier and ordering commissions

In a later phase, KasiPilot could connect traders to wholesalers or distributors.

Possible revenue sources:

- Commission on completed orders.
- Referral fees from suppliers.
- Transaction fees.
- Sponsored product placement.
- Distributor subscription fees.

This feature should be treated as a future revenue stream rather than part of the first prototype.

## 7. White-label partnerships

A distributor, bank, NGO, or ESD programme could license a branded version of KasiPilot.

They could receive:

- A customised interface.
- Branded WhatsApp communication.
- Custom reports.
- Partner-specific workflows.
- Dedicated administration.
- Integration with internal systems.

Revenue could come from:

- Setup fees.
- Annual licensing fees.
- Monthly platform fees.
- Support and integration charges.


## 8. Implementation and training fees

KasiPilot could charge institutions for:

- Trader onboarding.
- Training workshops.
- Data migration.
- System configuration.
- Custom dashboard development.
- Integration with existing systems.
- Ongoing technical support.

This is useful for generating early revenue while the recurring subscription model is still developing.

## 9. Sponsored access

Corporates, NGOs, government agencies, and foundations could sponsor access for groups of traders.

They may pay to support:

- Women-owned businesses.
- Youth-owned businesses.
- Township retailers.
- Rural traders.
- Businesses receiving grants.
- Businesses participating in an ESD programme.

The sponsor receives programme reports, while traders access the service without paying directly.

## Most suitable revenue streams by stage

| Stage | Main revenue source |
| :-- | :-- |
| Hackathon | No revenue; prove the prototype |
| Pilot | Sponsored access from a distributor or ESD partner |
| Early commercial phase | Per-trader distributor subscriptions |
| Growth phase | ESD contracts, partner dashboards, and integrations |
| Mature platform | Premium subscriptions, ordering commissions, and aggregated insights |

## Recommended first revenue hypothesis

For the first pilot, test this:

> **An FMCG distributor or ESD programme pays a monthly fee to provide KasiPilot to a defined group of traders and receives aggregated retailer insights and business-readiness reports.**

This model is attractive because:

- Traders receive the service at low or no cost.
- The partner already has access to the target market.
- The partner benefits from better information.
- KasiPilot avoids relying on low-income users as the only source of revenue.
- The pilot can measure a clear return on investment.


## Revenue model example

Assume a distributor sponsors 50 traders.

The distributor could pay for:

- Trader onboarding.
- Access to a partner dashboard.
- Stock and demand insights.
- Monthly reporting.
- Support and maintenance.

KasiPilot’s revenue would then be:

```text
Monthly platform fee
+ onboarding fee
+ support fee
+ optional integration fee
```

The exact price should be validated through interviews and pilot negotiations rather than presented as a confirmed market price.

## WhatsApp cost consideration

WhatsApp platform costs may affect margins, especially as usage grows. Meta states that businesses can access the first 1,000 conversations per month free of charge, after which conversation-related pricing applies; providers may also charge additional fees.  KasiPilot should therefore track messaging costs per active trader and include them in partner pricing.[^2]

## Business canvas version

> **Revenue streams:** Distributor-sponsored subscriptions, ESD programme contracts, partner dashboards, Readiness Passport and business-readiness services, premium trader subscriptions, supplier-order commissions, white-label licensing, implementation and training fees, and sponsored access from corporates, NGOs, banks, and government programmes.

## Short presentation version

> KasiPilot will primarily earn revenue through distributor and ESD programme partnerships. These organisations can pay per active trader for onboarding, business dashboards, stock insights, monitoring, and Readiness Passports. Additional revenue may come from premium trader subscriptions, supplier-order commissions, white-label licensing, implementation fees, and sponsored access. The basic trader service should remain free or subsidised initially to encourage adoption.
