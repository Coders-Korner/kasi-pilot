## Customer segments

KasiPilot serves two main customer groups: the **informal traders who use the product** and the **organisations that may pay for the service or benefit from its data**.

## Primary customer segment

### Informal traders and spaza-shop owners

These are the main users of KasiPilot.

They include:

- Spaza-shop owners.
- Township convenience-store owners.
- Informal food vendors.
- Kota and street-food sellers.
- Fruit and vegetable traders.
- Home-based retailers.
- Small-scale township wholesalers.
- Micro-businesses that sell products directly to local communities.


### Characteristics of the primary user

The ideal early user:

- Runs a small informal or semi-formal business.
- Uses WhatsApp regularly.
- Records sales manually or relies on memory.
- Has limited time for administration.
- Wants to understand sales and stock better.
- Experiences stock shortages or cash-flow uncertainty.
- May need business records to apply for funding or support.
- May prefer voice notes or local-language communication.

Spaza-shop owners are an appropriate starting segment because research identifies inadequate financial records, limited management skills, difficulty accessing finance, and weak bulk-buying networks as challenges affecting their growth.[^1]

## Secondary customer segments

### 1. FMCG distributors

These businesses supply products to spaza shops and informal retailers.

They may use KasiPilot to:

- Understand what products retailers need.
- Improve order collection.
- Identify fast-moving products.
- Reduce manual field visits.
- Improve stock replenishment.
- Build stronger relationships with retailers.


### 2. Wholesalers and cash-and-carry suppliers

These suppliers may use the platform to understand demand from small retailers and improve their sales and distribution processes.

Potential value includes:

- More accurate retailer orders.
- Demand forecasting.
- Customer segmentation.
- Product and stock insights.
- Better delivery planning.


### 3. Banks and alternative lenders

Financial institutions may benefit from consent-based business records that help them understand the activity and consistency of informal enterprises.

They could use KasiPilot to:

- Assess business activity.
- Support responsible lending decisions.
- Identify businesses ready for financial products.
- Monitor funded businesses.
- Reduce dependence on incomplete paper records.

KasiPilot should not promise automatic loans. It should provide better evidence that a lender can use alongside its normal assessment process.

### 4. Enterprise Supplier Development programmes

These programmes support small businesses through funding, training, market access, and business-development services.

They may use KasiPilot to:

- Monitor supported businesses.
- Track sales and business growth.
- Identify businesses needing additional support.
- Measure programme outcomes.
- Generate business-readiness reports.

This segment is relevant because South Africa’s Spaza Shop Support Fund includes financial and non-financial support such as stock, infrastructure, systems, business skills, digital literacy, and compliance training.[^2][^3]

### 5. Government and development agencies

Potential customers or partners include:

- Small Enterprise Development Agency.
- Small Enterprise Finance Agency.
- National Empowerment Fund.
- Municipal small-business programmes.
- Township and rural entrepreneurship initiatives.
- NGOs supporting informal businesses.
- Community development organisations.

They may use the platform to improve programme implementation, provide business support, or track the outcomes of funding initiatives.

### 6. Trader associations and community business networks

These organisations can help KasiPilot reach traders and support onboarding.

They may use the platform to:

- Offer members a shared business-support tool.
- Improve communication with traders.
- Identify common business challenges.
- Connect members to suppliers and funders.
- Coordinate training and support.


### 7. Corporate procurement and supplier-development teams

Large companies may use KasiPilot to identify, support, and monitor township-based suppliers.

Potential value includes:

- Supplier-readiness monitoring.
- Progress reporting.
- Procurement pipeline development.
- Evidence of enterprise-development impact.
- Identification of businesses that could become formal suppliers.


## Customer priority

Do not target every segment at the same time. For the hackathon, present the following priority:


| Priority | Segment | Role |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 1 | Spaza shops and informal traders | Main users |
| 2 | FMCG distributor | Potential paying pilot partner |
| 3 | Trader association | Distribution and onboarding partner |
| 4 | ESD programme or funder | Future paying customer |
| 5 | Banks and government agencies | Longer-term partners |

## Best initial target

The clearest initial customer segment is:

> **Spaza-shop owners and informal food retailers in a selected township who use WhatsApp and need a simple way to record sales, monitor stock, and build credible business records.**

For the first pilot, narrow this further:

> **Twenty to fifty spaza-shop owners in one township, supported by one distributor or trader association.**

This focused segment is easier to reach, interview, onboard, and measure than the entire informal economy.

## Users versus paying customers

It is important to distinguish between the person using KasiPilot and the organisation paying for it.

### User

The informal trader uses KasiPilot to:

- Record sales.
- Track stock.
- View business summaries.
- Receive alerts.
- Build a business history.


### Potential payer

A distributor, ESD programme, bank, NGO, or government initiative pays because it wants to:

- Improve retailer support.
- Monitor business performance.
- Understand demand.
- Assess supplier readiness.
- Measure programme impact.


## Business canvas version

> **Customer segments:** Informal traders, spaza-shop owners, street-food vendors, and township retailers as primary users; FMCG distributors, wholesalers, banks, alternative lenders, ESD programmes, government agencies, NGOs, trader associations, and corporate procurement teams as secondary customers, partners, or paying organisations.

## Short presentation version

> KasiPilot targets informal traders and spaza-shop owners who need a simple way to record sales and manage stock. Its secondary customers include FMCG distributors, wholesalers, banks, ESD programmes, government agencies, NGOs, and trader associations that need better visibility into informal businesses and the ability to support them more effectively.
